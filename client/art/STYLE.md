# Slavian art style

The style guide every piece of art follows, written with the
game-assets-enhancement skill (`.claude/skills/game-assets-enhancement`).
The sizes, the replacement process and the interface are in
`docs/art-style.md`.

**Style:** painted, outline-free pieces with soft shading, as seen from above
at a slant, all lit by one warm late-afternoon sun from the upper left
(north-west). They are vector shapes with gradients, generated by
`tools/art/generate.py`, and any piece can be swapped for a painted PNG
through `art.json`.

## Semantic colour

These never change:

- **Green** is your own: your outlines, your banners, and your actions in the interface (malachite).
- **Blue** is an ally.
- **Red (madder)** is the enemy: hordes, their outlines and their medallion rims.
- **Gold** is reward and rank: our unit medallions, levels and ember.
- **Amber** is time running out.

## Lighting rules

1. **Shared warm undertone.** Highlights lean warm (`#fff1b0`, `#fbf5e6`) and
   shadows lean cool and dark (`#2b2118` at low opacity), never neutral grey.
2. **One light direction: upper left.**
   - Gradients run light at the top left to dark at the bottom right.
   - Soft shadows fall to the lower right.
   - The ground shader lights its relief from the north-west too (`light = (-0.62, -0.78)`).
3. **Real value range.** Each piece has a lit plane and a shadow plane, not one
   mid-tone; roofs, walls and medallions all have both.
4. **Aerial perspective.** The map is seen from above, so depth comes from
   height and from the cloud layer instead of distant hills:
   - Features cast soft shadows.
   - The ground's relief rises with the terrain.
   - Cloud shadows float above the ground and slide a little faster than it when the map pans.
5. **Code light grade** (`MapGrade`, `shaders/grade.gdshader`). It goes over the
   composed map and fuses the separately drawn pieces into one lit world:
   - a low warm wash from the upper left;
   - a gentle vignette round the part of the map in view;
   - the drifting cloud shadows.

## The layer stack (the map)

Back to front, in one place: `HexMap` and `game_screen._build_ui`.

1. **Ground** (`MapGround`): one mesh where terrains blend, with grain, relief and water sheen.
2. **Features** (`MapFeatures`): woods, peaks, hills, reeds and stones, each its own
   sprite, placed by a seeded hash (variant, jitter, size, skip). Hexes
   holding a settlement, camp or site stay clear.
   - Features never look like anything the player acts on: no huts, no tents, no banners.
3. **Rivers, roads, the grid.**
4. **Playfield pieces:** settlements, camps, forts, outposts, sites, armies, caravans and hordes. Each is its own sprite with its owner's outline.
5. **Light grade** (`MapGrade`, canvas layer 1): warm wash, cloud shadows (parallax 1.15) and the vignette.
6. **Interface** (canvas layer 2).

## Sprite manifest

Every object is its own file, with no text baked in; words are drawn by code.

- **Features:** blossom (hallowed meadows; it replaced a menhir that could pass for a stone idol site), bush, dark pine, grass, hill, ice, mountain, oak, peak, pine, reeds.
- **Settlements:**
  - villages in five stages, the ruins and the Holy City;
  - banner pole and cloth, which take the owner's colour.
- **On the map:** bandit camp, lair, fort, outpost.
- **Ancient sites:** kurgan, sunken shrine, old hillfort, stone idol, frozen ruin.
- **Our units** (gold rim): spearman, archer, horseman, scout, settler, forest archer, river barge, border rider.
- **Enemy units** (iron rim run with madder; shapes reused where kinds share them):
  - North: grey wolf, Leshy's thrall, cave bear, volkolak, upyr, likho, zmey whelp.
  - East: steppe raider, horse archer, lancer, steppe shaman, Khan's guard, scaling ladder.
  - The Order: zealot, crossbowman, knight-brother, inquisitor, siege ram, catapult, siege tower.
- **Factions:** the North, the East, the Order.
- **Resources:** wood, stone, grain, iron, ember.
- **Village:**
  - ten buildings and six wall works in three stages each;
  - the walls by material (wood, timber, stone, fortress);
  - four fields in three stages;
  - the empty and locked plots.
- **Interface:** ten line icons (tinted by the theme), the start picture and the app icon.

## Still to do with the skill's generation pipeline

The skill paints sprites through fal.ai (`fal-ai/nano-banana-pro`, cut out
with `pixelcut/background-removal`). That needs a fal.ai account, its API key
and the fal.ai MCP server connected to Claude Code; none are set up here yet.
With them, the next pass would:

1. Paint a style anchor (village stage 3) to this guide.
2. Paint every manifest line against the anchor.
3. Trim each sprite and keep its footprint (128×128 for most pieces).
4. Point the ids at the PNGs in `art.json`.

The generated SVGs stay as the fallback and as the composition reference.
